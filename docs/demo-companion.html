Step 4 · Sentiment Score — spend time here

FinBERT — how it works

❌ VADER (general NLP)

"Beat estimates but cut guidance" → reads NEGATIVE because of the word "cut"

Rule-based word dictionary. No context awareness.

✅ FinBERT (financial BERT)

Same sentence → reads POSITIVE. Understands earnings language, guidance context.

Fine-tuned on 100k+ Reuters, earnings calls, Financial PhraseBank. 87% accuracy vs VADER's 65%.

Output per article

Positive0.82
Negative0.10
Neutral0.08

Compound: +0.72  ·  Label: Positive

Exponential decay weighting

weight = e^(−λ × age_days) λ = ln(2) / 3  ← half-life 3 days avg = Σ(score×weight) / Σ(weights)

Today = full weight. 3 days ago = half. 6 days ago = quarter.

Step 4 · FinBERT — if asked for more depth

How BERT actually works

BERT = Bidirectional Transformer

  • Reads entire sentence both directions at once
  • Not left-to-right like older RNNs
  • "cut" means different things depending on surrounding words — BERT knows this
  • Pre-trained on Wikipedia + books (general English)

Fine-tuning = domain adaptation

  • Take pre-trained BERT
  • Continue training on financial text
  • Reuters, earnings calls, analyst reports, Financial PhraseBank
  • Result: model understands financial context

In production

  • Singleton — loads once (440MB), stays in memory
  • Batch inference — 32 articles at once, ~10× faster
  • Auto-detects GPU (Apple MPS / CUDA) → CPU fallback
  • First call 2–5s. After Redis cache: <100ms

Accuracy benchmark

FinBERT: 87%

Step 5 · Narrative Velocity

Rate of attention change — not just volume

"Velocity measures how fast coverage is changing, not just how much there is. Spikes in velocity often precede price reversals — retail attention peaks right before the narrative collapses."

The formula

velocity = (count_last_24h − count_prior_24h) / max(count_prior_24h, 1) × 100 e.g. 10 → 20 articles = velocity +100 20 → 10 articles = velocity −50
Accelerating >+20% Stable ±20% Decelerating <−20%

Why it matters

  • High velocity + high hype = overheated narrative
  • Attention spikes are leading, not lagging indicators
  • GME 2021: velocity hit extreme before price peaked

What to say

"Volume alone doesn't tell you direction. A stock with 5 articles is fine. The same stock going from 5 to 50 articles overnight is a different story."

Step 6 · Hype Score

3-Factor model — 0 to 100

Factor 1 · Imbalance

Up to 40 pts

(dominant_ratio − 0.5) × 2 × 40

90% bullish = suspicious. Healthy = balanced.

Factor 2 · Velocity

Up to 35 pts

min(velocity/100, 1.0) × 35

Fast attention is itself a hype signal.

Factor 3 · Concentration

Up to 25 pts

max(0, 1 − sources/total) × 25

2 outlets ≠ 20 independent confirmations.

🟢 Low

< 20

🔵 Moderate

20 – 45

🟡 High

45 – 70

🔴 Extreme

> 70

GameStop Jan 2021 would have scored Extreme on all 3 factors simultaneously.

Step 7 · Pattern Archetype

6 narrative archetypes — rule-based, fully explainable

🔥 Short Squeeze Dynamics — Extreme

Compound >+0.4 · Accelerating · Imbalance >75% · Hype >65. GME/AMC pattern.

😨 Macro Fear — High

Sustained negative <−0.2 · Broad multi-source · Hype >45. Fed decisions, regulation.

🌊 Theme/Sector Wave — High

Positive · Accelerating · Diverse sources · Hype >40. AI boom, clean energy.

📉 Narrative Cool-off — Moderate

Velocity decelerating · Was high hype · Coverage thinning. Story fading.

⏳ Pre-Catalyst Build — Moderate

Mentions accelerating · Tone stays neutral. Earnings/FDA/product launch coming.

✅ Balanced Coverage — Low

No unusual signals. Diversified sources. Healthy information flow.

Rule-based not ML → fully explainable. We show users exactly which conditions fired. No black box.

Step 9 · Narrative Timeline

91-day drift chart — how the story evolved

"Most tools show today's sentiment. We show you the whole 91-day story. We also detect exactly when the narrative shifted direction and annotate it on the chart."

How drift detection works

  • Daily aggregate = weighted avg of all article scores that day
  • Rolling windows: 7d · 30d · 90d
  • Compare window avg to prior window avg
  • If shift > 0.15 compound points → flag as narrative shift
  • Annotate the date on the chart

Window stats below chart

Technical deep dive — if asked

End-to-end backend pipeline

Background (Celery — every 30 min)

  • Celery Beat fires one task per active ticker
  • Tasks run in parallel (worker pool)
  • Scrapers: RSS (∞) · GNews (100/day) · Reddit
  • Dedup by URL — DB unique constraint
  • Batch FinBERT on new articles (32 at a time)
  • Store SentimentScore per Narrative
  • Create PriceImpact → schedule 1h/4h/24h price checks

On-demand (per request)

  • Check Redis → if hit, return <100ms
  • If miss: query last 20 articles from DB
  • Score any unscored articles via FinBERT
  • Compute weighted avg sentiment
  • Compute velocity (SQL aggregation)
  • Compute 3-factor hype score
  • Classify pattern (rule-based)
  • Cache result 15 min → return

Cache TTLs

Quote: 10m · Chart: 10m · Analysis: 15m · Drift: 30m · Summary: 15m · Search: 30m

Q&A — keep this slide up

Quick answers for tough questions

Different from Yahoo Finance?

They show charts + articles. We add: sentiment scoring, velocity, 3-factor hype, 6 pattern archetypes, 91-day drift. A narrative layer that doesn't exist there.

Why not just ChatGPT?

ChatGPT gives prose. We need structured numbers — compound scores, velocity %, hype 0–100 — to chart, trend, and compare over 91 days. LLMs only used where prose is right output.

Isn't predicting markets impossible?

Yes — which is why we don't. No price predictions. ML classifier is experimental + labeled as such. We explain narratives, not forecast prices.

FinBERT accuracy?

87% on Financial PhraseBank vs VADER 65% / general BERT 72%. Handles earnings language, guidance cuts, analyst upgrades — patterns VADER misses entirely.

Biggest technical challenge?

Running 440MB FinBERT without per-request lag. Fixed with: singleton loader + batch inference (32×) + Redis caching. Most requests never touch the model.

Why rule-based patterns not ML?

Explainability + no labeled training data. Users see exactly which conditions fired. Can migrate to ML when we have thousands of labeled examples.
