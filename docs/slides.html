"Every 30 minutes, Celery — our background task scheduler — scrapes articles from 4 sources: RSS feeds which are unlimited, GNews, Reddit, and Twelve Data for prices. Articles get deduplicated by URL so we never score the same one twice. New articles go straight through FinBERT in batches of 32. Scores get stored. When a user searches a stock, we check Redis cache first — if it's there, response is under 100ms. If not, we run the full pipeline which takes 2–5 seconds the first time, then cache it for 15 minutes."

Background (Celery)

On-demand (per request)

Check Redis first. Cache miss → run full pipeline (~2–5s first time). Cache hit → instant (<100ms).

"FinBERT is BERT — Google's transformer model — but fine-tuned on over 100,000 financial documents. Reuters news, earnings call transcripts, analyst reports. The difference matters: VADER is a rule-based word dictionary, it counts positive and negative words with no context. FinBERT reads the whole sentence bidirectionally, so it understands that 'beat estimates but cut guidance' is bullish, not negative. It outputs three probabilities per article — positive, negative, neutral — and a compound score from -1 to +1. We then apply exponential decay so today's articles count more than last week's. Half-life is 3 days. 87% accuracy on the standard financial benchmark vs VADER's 65%."

"The Hype Score is our most original contribution — a 3-factor model, 0 to 100. Factor 1: Sentiment Imbalance — up to 40 points. If 90% of articles are bullish, that's suspicious. Healthy stocks have mixed, balanced coverage. Factor 2: Velocity — up to 35 points. Fast-moving attention is itself a hype signal. Even accurate positive coverage can be hype if it's accelerating too fast. Factor 3: Source Concentration — up to 25 points. 20 articles from 2 websites isn't independent confirmation, it's the same story re-syndicated. We penalize that. Add them up: under 20 is Low, 20–45 Moderate, 45–70 High, above 70 is Extreme. GameStop in January 2021 would have hit Extreme on all three factors."

Hype Score — 3 Factors, 0 to 100

Factor 1 · Imbalance

Up to 40 points

90% bullish coverage is suspicious. Healthy stocks have balanced reporting.

(dominant_ratio − 0.5) × 2 × 40

Factor 2 · Velocity

Up to 35 points

Fast-moving attention is itself a hype signal, independent of content.

min(velocity / 100, 1.0) × 35

Factor 3 · Concentration

Up to 25 points

20 articles from 2 sites ≠ 20 independent signals. That's syndication.

max(0, 1 − sources/total) × 25

"On top of the hype score, we classify every stock's narrative into one of 6 archetypes. We chose rule-based over machine learning on purpose — we wanted full explainability. Users can see exactly which conditions triggered the label, no black box. Short Squeeze Dynamics is the most extreme — that's your GameStop setup. Macro Fear is broad negative coverage around systemic events. Theme/Sector Wave is an overheated sector rotation. Narrative Cool-off is a story that's fading. Pre-Catalyst Build is quiet anticipation before an earnings or product launch. Balanced Coverage is the healthy baseline. Point out: we check in priority order, most extreme first, fallback to Balanced Coverage if nothing fires."

6 Narrative Archetypes — Explainable, Rule-Based

🔥 Short Squeeze Dynamics Extreme

Extreme bullish + accelerating velocity + 75%+ imbalance + hype >65. GME/AMC pattern.

😨 Macro Fear High

Sustained negative + broad multi-source coverage + hype >45. Fed, regulation, market anxiety.

🌊 Theme/Sector Wave High

Positive + accelerating + diverse sources + hype >40. AI boom, clean energy cycles.

📉 Narrative Cool-off Moderate

Velocity decelerating + was high hype + coverage thinning. Story fading, attention moving on.

⏳ Pre-Catalyst Build Moderate

Mentions accelerating + neutral tone. Earnings/FDA/product launch anticipation.

✅ Balanced Coverage Low

No unusual signals. Diversified sources. Routine, healthy information flow.

Rule-based (not ML) → fully explainable. Users see exactly which conditions triggered the label.
